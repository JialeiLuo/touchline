# Touchline: first-time participant tasks

Status: Batch 9 was accepted and closed by the owner on 27 October 2026. This sheet is retained as the review protocol; it is not a request for further participation.

Use the current local review build at http://127.0.0.1:8769 on the computer running Touchline. The older public demo does not contain these changes. If the participant is elsewhere, arrange a session on this computer; do not use the old public demo as evidence about this build.

This is a 10–15 minute review of a fictional football prototype. We are testing the product, not the participant. Explain what you are looking for and what you think the screen means. There are no preferred opinions. Do not read the facilitator notes before trying the tasks.

## Tasks — give this section to the participant

1. **Catch up.** Imagine you missed minutes 55–66 of the built-in match. Use Touchline to understand that spell. In your own words, what happened, and what does the explanation help you understand?
2. **Check a claim.** Pick one claim from the explanation and find its supporting source. What is a recorded fact, what is an interpretation, and what remains uncertain? Does the substitution prove that the team improved? Why or why not?
3. **Make it yours.** Find Ellis in Teams & lineups and follow that player. Compare Quick and Tactical for the same interval. What changes? What should stay the same?
4. **Recover.** With the replay around minute 66, try an interval ending at minute 80. Explain the message and recover to a usable interval. Then find a way to see the recorded events without relying on online analysis.
5. **Understand a term.** Open an underlined football term and try a different glossary language. What changed language, and what did not?
6. **Give your verdict.** What was confusing? Did Touchline add anything beyond the statistics? When would you use it, if ever? What would you change first?

If stuck, say what you expected and where you looked. It is fine to abandon a task. Do not keep retrying online explanations to make the product look better; failures and local fallbacks are useful observations.

## Facilitator notes — keep separate until the tasks are attempted

Start on a freshly loaded page, with the built-in match and no completed explanation. Let the participant discover the example or replay controls. Do not show the guide unless they choose it. If they are stuck after about two minutes, ask what they expected; give a hint only if they want one and mark the task assisted. Timing is optional, not a performance claim.

The same 55–66 interval should remain selected when comparing modes. Team/player focus changes priority, not source facts; important incidents remain included. A report or temporal association does not prove causation. Future intervals must be rejected until revealed. Glossary language changes definitions, not the English commentary. Local explanations and the event recap remain useful when online analysis is unavailable; record which delivery the participant actually saw.

Capture discoveries even if the participant reaches the destination: comprehension and source-finding matter, not only successful clicks. One participant is a small formative sample, not proof of broad usability or superiority. The builder's walkthrough and browser tests are separate evidence.
